Privacy Policy — Euphysis

Euphysis is made by Laminae Limited (NZBN 9429053217417), a New Zealand registered charity (CC64719).

This is the privacy policy for the Android app Euphysis, package nz.co.laminae.field, version 0.5.0 (build 13). Aesthesia, the living field, is Euphysis's first app.

  • Effective date: the day this build is released to testers
  • Last updated: 30 October 2026
  • Policy version: 2.0
  • Where to read it: in the app, under Privacy in the menu, and on our website at laminae.co.nz/privacy.
  • Privacy officer: Tim, the founder of Laminae Limited.

On Google Play the developer shown is Laminae's founder: Laminae Limited makes Euphysis, and its founder's Google Play account publishes it until Laminae's own account is set up.

Euphysis is in a closed test. This policy describes the build named above. When the app changes, this policy changes with it, before the new build reaches anyone.

In short

Euphysis draws a field of light on the screen and can play it as sound. Your finger drives the field: you touch it, draw shapes in it and fill them. The sound in the room can drive it too, if you choose the microphone in the field's settings.

Euphysis collects nothing about you. It keeps what you make on your phone, and uses it only for what you ask the app to do. It has no internet permission, so it cannot send anything to us or to anyone else. There are no adverts and no analytics, and no crash is ever reported by itself. There is no account and no sign-in.

Three things can leave the app's own storage, and each only when you choose it: a plain .wav copy of a take you record, if you switch the plain copy on, which goes into your phone's shared Music/Laminae folder; an email you write and send yourself with Send feedback; and, where the app has About, a short crash note or your feedback, only when you press Share crash report or Send there.

What the app listens toThe microphone, only while you have the sound switched on.
What it works out from moment to momentA level, a peak, how much of what it hears is the app's own sound coming back, and how much sits on the notes the field is playing. These numbers move the field if you have chosen the microphone, and are then gone. They are not written down.
What it keepsYour settings, the configs you keep and the history of their changes; a take you record; a copy of an audio file you choose in the Mixer. All on the phone.
What the app sends over a networkNothing. It holds no network permission.
What can leave the app's controlThe plain .wav in Music/Laminae, because it sits in shared storage where the rest of your phone can reach it; an email you choose to send us.

Sealed in this policy means encrypted so that only this phone can read it.

Moving light

The field moves and changes brightness all the time, and parts of it can brighten quickly. If you have photosensitive epilepsy, or flashing or moving light affects you, take care, or do not use the field. If you feel unwell while watching, look away and stop. The first screen of the app says this before the field moves, and lets you reduce motion; you can change it later under Dials, Colour.

What the microphone is used for

This matters more than any other part of the policy, so it is set out in full.

While the sound is on, or while you have chosen the microphone to drive the field, the app has the microphone open. From each short slice of audio it works out four numbers: how loud the slice is, how high its peak is, how much of it is the app's own output coming back through the speaker, and what share of it sits at the notes the field is playing. It does not keep the sound, and it does not keep those numbers.

Those numbers move the field only if you choose the microphone. In the field's settings, under Chaos from, the choice starts on The field's own generator. If you choose The microphone, a loud, sharp sound (a clap, a knock, a tap) puts a burst of energy into the field at a random place, in proportion to how hard it was, and the steady change in the room's level acts on the field as a kind of wind. The choice goes back to the field's own generator each time the app starts. While the sound is on and the microphone is not chosen, the microphone is open and nothing from it moves the field.

Nothing from the microphone is stored, except a take you record. This build has no audio check: the Diagnostics screen that ran it is left out of the tester build.

A recorded take is different. If you use the mixer to record, real audio is written to a file. See "Recording, and the plain copy" below.

Where your data is stored, and how it is protected

Everything the app keeps is written into the app's own private folder on the phone, except a plain .wav copy if you ask for one. There is no server, no account and no sign-in.

Settings and configs. The app's settings, the configs you keep and the history of their changes are kept unsealed in the app's private folder, which other apps cannot open. They describe the field and its sound, not you.

Takes are sealed. The key is made on the phone the first time the app runs and is held in Android's encrypted storage, backed by the phone's own keystore. It never leaves the phone and is not known to Laminae Limited.

A file you choose in the Mixer. The Mixer's Another file on the phone… opens Android's own file picker. The audio file you choose is copied into the app's private cache on the phone, so a channel can play it. It is read on the phone and is not sent anywhere. Android may clear that cache by itself; the file you chose stays where it was.

Timers. If a timer series is turned on (on the What is collected screen) and a run is started with the timer command, the app stores three numbers for that run: time elapsed, time remaining and progress. They are a clock, not anything about you, so they are kept unsealed. The switch goes back to off each time the app starts.

Recording, and the plain copy

When you record a take in the mixer, the app writes a sealed recording inside its own private folder. That file can only be read with the key on your phone. A take stops by itself after five minutes, and while one is recording the app shows a recording badge with the time.

The mixer has a single plain copy switch, and it starts off.

  • With it off, the take exists only in sealed form. To play a take back, the app writes a temporary readable copy inside its own private folder, which no other app can reach, plays it, and deletes it as soon as playback ends. Nothing is written to shared storage.
  • With it on, the app also writes an ordinary .wav file into your phone's shared Music/Laminae collection, so you can find it in a music or files app. That file is not encrypted. A working copy is made while the file is written and is deleted immediately afterwards.

What the plain copy means in practice. Once a .wav is in the shared Music collection it is an ordinary file on your phone:

  • any other app that has permission to read audio or media can read it;
  • any cloud-sync, file-manager or backup app you have installed can copy it off the phone;
  • it stays on the phone if you uninstall Euphysis or clear its data.

Euphysis having no internet permission does not limit what the rest of your phone does with that file. If you would rather no readable copy existed outside the app, leave the plain copy switch off.

Other people's voices. A recording made in a shared room may capture other people. Euphysis does not know who is in the room and cannot ask them. If you record other people, it is for you to tell them and to handle the recording accordingly.

Send feedback

In the app's menu (or in About, where it is there), Send feedback opens your own email app with a message addressed to developer-feedback@laminae.co.nz. The draft's subject names the app and its version; its body holds the app's version and your Android version, and nothing else. You write the rest. Nothing is sent unless you send it, from your email app and your email account.

Crash report and feedback in About. If the app meets an error it did not expect, it keeps a short note of it in its private folder on the phone: when it happened, and codes for the kind of error and the part of the app it came from. It never keeps the error's message, anything you typed, a file name or anything about you, and it keeps only the most recent ones. What you write in Send feedback in About is kept there too, as a draft, until you press Send. Neither leaves the phone unless you send it: Share crash report and Send open Android's share sheet, where you choose the app and where it goes, and each carries the app's version and build and nothing else you did not add.

Controlling the app from a computer, in this test build

This test build lets a computer connected to the phone control the app. It is protected by a code made once when the app is installed.

You should know three things about it:

  1. The code is kept unencrypted in the app's own private folder, which other apps cannot open. The Commands screen that showed it is left out of this build.
  2. The code is written to the phone's system log when the app starts, and again every time a wrong code is offered. A computer connected to the phone with USB debugging turned on can read that log, so it can learn the code. The answers to commands, including any stored values read back, are written to the same log.
  3. Every command ships, including the ones that read, export and erase what the app has stored.

So, in this test build, an app on your phone that holds the code, or a computer with USB debugging access to your phone, can read what Euphysis has stored. That is a weakness of the test build, not an intended feature, and it is on the list of things to fix before the release.

The vault

The vault, a place for secrets typed in by hand, is left out of this build: its screen is not in the menu, and nothing in the app asks you for a secret. Two traces of it remain. Android's quick-settings panel can list a tile named Vault, which opens the app and nothing more; and the app still declares the fingerprint or screen-lock permission, which it never asks you to use.

Backups

Android's automatic backup is turned off for this app, and the app excludes every part of its storage from cloud backup and from moving to a new phone. So nothing in Euphysis's private folder is copied to your Google account, and nothing is carried across when you set up a new phone.

If you use a backup or file-sync app of your own that copies your Music folder, a plain .wav from Music/Laminae will be caught by it. That copy is outside the app's control and is governed by whatever service you used.

Testing, and what reaches us

During the test, the only information that reaches Laminae Limited is what you choose to send us, such as feedback by email. We use it only to find patterns that help us make the app better for you. We remove anything that identifies you before we analyse it, and we delete it once the analysis is done. There is never a shared central store of your data.

If you are a tester, you gave us the email address of your Google Play account. It goes into Google Play's tester list, so Google can let you install the test, and we take it off whenever you ask. Google handles the tester list and anything you choose to send through Play. That is Google's process, not the app's.

What this app never does

  • It never sends your data over a network. It holds no network permission, and no networking or upload code is written into it.
  • It shows no advertising and contains no advertising library.
  • It collects no analytics and reports no crashes to us by itself.
  • It has no accounts, no sign-in and no password.
  • It does not read your location, your calendar, your health data or which other apps you use.
  • It makes no health claim and has no health features.
  • It does not sell your information. It shares nothing with us or anyone else; the only other way in is the computer connection described above.

How long things are kept, and how to delete them

What the app keeps stays on your phone until you remove it. The app does not expire it for you.

The plain answer: uninstalling Euphysis removes everything the app keeps in its own storage; a plain .wav you exported to Music/Laminae is yours to delete in your phone's own Files or Music app.

What you doWhat it removesWhat it leaves

There is no single button in the app that removes everything. The simplest way is to delete any exported .wav files and then uninstall the app.

You do not need to ask us to delete anything, because we hold nothing of yours from the app. If you want that confirmed in writing, write to developer-feedback@laminae.co.nz.

Children

Euphysis is not directed at children. On Google Play it is declared for an audience of 18 and over.

The app has no accounts, no adverts, no purchases and no messaging, and nobody can contact a person through it.

Before the microphone is used

Before Android asks for the microphone, Euphysis shows a short screen, The sound in the room, saying what it listens for and what it keeps. Continue goes on to Android's own dialogue; Not now, the back button or a tap outside the screen leaves the sound off and asks nothing. If you decline Android's dialogue, the sound does not start and the rest of the app works as before.

Your rights, and New Zealand law

Laminae Limited is a New Zealand company and registered charity, and is the agency responsible for this app under the Privacy Act 2020. Its privacy officer is Tim, the founder.

Because Euphysis holds nothing of yours on any system of ours, there is normally nothing for us to give you access to or to correct. Everything the app holds is on your own phone, where you can read it and delete it yourself, as described above.

If you believe we hold personal information about you, such as an email you sent us, you may ask for access to it and for correction of it. Write to developer-feedback@laminae.co.nz. We will answer within the time the Privacy Act allows.

If you are not satisfied with our answer, you may complain to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner (privacy.org.nz).

If something goes wrong. If we become aware of a privacy breach affecting personal information we hold, and that breach is likely to cause serious harm, we will notify the Office of the Privacy Commissioner and the people affected, as the Privacy Act 2020 requires. Because the app sends us nothing, the realistic scope of such a breach is what you have sent us directly, such as an email.
