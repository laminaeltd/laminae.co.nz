Privacy Policy — Euphysis

Euphysis is made by Laminae Limited (NZBN 9429053217417), a New Zealand registered charity (CC64719).

This is the privacy policy for the Android app Euphysis, package nz.co.laminae.field, version 0.5.0 (build 13). Aesthesia, the living field, is Euphysis's first app.

  • Effective date: the day this build is released to testers
  • Last updated: 15 November 2026
  • Policy version: 2.1
  • Where to read it: in the app, under Privacy in the menu, and on our website at laminae.co.nz/privacy.
  • Privacy officer: Tim, the founder of Laminae Limited.

On Google Play the developer shown is Laminae's founder: Laminae Limited makes Euphysis, and its founder's Google Play account publishes it until Laminae's own account is set up.

Euphysis is in a closed test. This policy describes the build named above. When the app changes, this policy changes with it. The website has the new version first. The app carries it from the next build.

In short

Euphysis draws a field of light on the screen and can play it as sound. Your finger drives the field: you touch it, draw shapes in it and fill them. The sound in the room can drive it too, if you choose the microphone in the field's settings.

Euphysis collects nothing about you. It keeps what you make on your phone, and uses it only for what you ask the app to do. It has no internet permission, so it cannot send anything to us or to anyone else. There are no adverts. Nothing about how you use the app is sent anywhere, and no crash is ever reported by itself. There is no account and no sign-in.

Four things can leave the app's own storage, and each only when you choose it. A plain .wav copy of a take or sample you record, if you switch the plain copy on, goes into your phone's shared Music/Laminae folder. A .mid sequence you save or make a variant of goes into Music/Laminae/Sequences; the app also puts example sequences there. Where the app has About, a short crash note or your feedback leaves only when you press Share crash report or Send there. Config text leaves only if you press Copy or Export on the Configs tab.

If you connect a MIDI device and send notes to it, the notes go to that device and nowhere else.

Moving light

The field moves and changes brightness all the time, and parts of it can brighten quickly. If you have photosensitive epilepsy, or flashing or moving light affects you, take care, or do not use the field. If you feel unwell while watching, look away and stop. The first screen of the app says this before the field moves, and lets you reduce motion; you can change it later under Dials, Colour.

What the microphone is used for

This matters more than any other part of the policy, so it is set out in full.

The microphone is open only while something is listening to it. That is the field, if you choose Mic or Aux audio under Chaos from, or a Mixer channel, if you set its source to Input. Playing sound on its own does not open it. From each short slice of audio it works out four numbers: how loud the slice is, how high its peak is, how much of it is the app's own output coming back through the speaker, and what share of it sits at the notes the field is playing. It does not keep the sound, and it does not keep those numbers.

Those numbers move the field only if you choose the microphone. In the field's settings, under Chaos from, the choice starts on The field's own generator. If you choose Mic, a loud, sharp sound (a clap, a knock, a tap) puts a burst of energy into the field at a random place, in proportion to how hard it was, and the steady change in the room's level acts on the field as a kind of wind. A microphone you chose is still chosen when you reopen the app, but it stays off until you tap it. While the microphone is not open, nothing from it can move the field.

A Mixer channel set to Input plays what the microphone hears through the speaker, and it goes into a take or a sample if you record one. The Input can be the phone's microphone or an audio device you plug in.

Nothing from the microphone is stored, except a take or a sample you record. This build has no audio check: the Diagnostics screen that ran it is left out of the tester build.

A recorded take is different. If you use the mixer to record, real audio is written to a file. See "Recording, and the plain copy" below.

MIDI

The Mixer can send the notes a channel plays to a MIDI device you have connected by USB or Bluetooth. If you choose, it also sends controller values and a clock. It can play notes that a device sends. The field can send its notes to a device too. Each channel can use a different device. The app needs no extra permission for this. It keeps the name your phone gives each device, so it can set the route again. Once a note has gone to another device, that device decides what happens to it.

Where your data is stored, and how it is protected

Everything the app keeps is written into the app's own private folder on the phone, except a plain .wav copy if you ask for one. There is no server, no account and no sign-in.

Settings, history and your own work. The app's settings, the configs you keep, the history of their changes (with the number each setting changed from and to), your saved drawings and picture lists, your section locks, the sequence variants you rate or keep, the record of when you last used each thing, and the names you give things are kept unsealed in the app's private folder, which other apps cannot open. They describe the field and its sound. Names you type are kept as you typed them, so do not put anything private in a name. Samples are sealed, as takes are, with a plain note beside each of its length, tempo, key, name and tags. This build has no activity journal.

Takes are sealed. The key is made on the phone the first time the app runs and is held in Android's encrypted storage, backed by the phone's own keystore. It never leaves the phone and is not known to Laminae Limited.

A file you choose in the Mixer. The Mixer's Another file on the phone… opens Android's own file picker. The file you choose is copied into the app's private folder, so the channel can be put back when you reopen the app. The picker's own temporary copy sits in the app's private cache. The copy is removed when no channel is using the file. It is not sealed, no other app can reach it, and it is not sent anywhere. The file you chose stays where it was.

Timers. If a timer series is turned on (on the What is collected screen) and a run is started with the timer command, the app stores three numbers for that run: time elapsed, time remaining and progress. They are a clock, not anything about you, so they are kept unsealed. The switch goes back to off each time the app starts.

Recording, and the plain copy

When you record a take in the mixer, the app writes a sealed recording inside its own private folder. That file can only be read with the key on your phone. A take stops by itself after five minutes, and while one is recording the app shows a recording badge with the time.

The mixer has a single plain copy switch, and it starts off.

  • With it off, the take exists only in sealed form. To play a take back, the app writes a temporary readable copy inside its own private folder, which no other app can reach, plays it, and deletes it as soon as playback ends. Nothing is written to shared storage.
  • With it on, the app also writes an ordinary .wav file into your phone's shared Music/Laminae collection, so you can find it in a music or files app. That file is not encrypted. A working copy is made while the file is written and is deleted immediately afterwards.

What the plain copy means in practice. Once a .wav is in the shared Music collection it is an ordinary file on your phone:

  • any other app that has permission to read audio or media can read it;
  • any cloud-sync, file-manager or backup app you have installed can copy it off the phone;
  • it stays on the phone if you uninstall Euphysis or clear its data.

Euphysis having no internet permission does not limit what the rest of your phone does with that file. If you would rather no readable copy existed outside the app, leave the plain copy switch off.

Samples, names and deleting. A sample is a short capture of the Mixer's sound, kept sealed in the same way as a take. If a channel is set to Input, a sample can hold what the microphone heard. If the plain copy switch is on, a sample gets a plain .wav in Music/Laminae too. A take you name gets a plain .wav with that name, and a saved sequence's .mid has the name you typed, so those names sit in shared storage. Deleting a take or a sample in the app moves the sealed file to the Bin. It never touches a plain .wav copy. If you delete a take to be rid of it, delete its plain copy in your Files or Music app as well.

Other people's voices. A recording made in a shared room may capture other people. Euphysis does not know who is in the room and cannot ask them. If you record other people, it is for you to tell them and to handle the recording accordingly.

Send feedback

Send feedback is in About in this build. It asks three short questions. What you type is kept on your phone as a draft, and it stays there, after you send it too, until you start a new one. When you press Send, the app hands your answers, with the app's version and build, to Android's share sheet, addressed to developer-feedback@laminae.co.nz. You choose the app. Nothing is sent unless you send it, from your own app and account. If the share sheet does not open, you can copy the text instead. (In a build without About, the menu's Send feedback opens your own email app with a draft that holds the app's version and your Android version, and nothing else.)

Crash report and feedback in About. If the app meets an error it did not expect, it keeps a short note of it in its private folder on the phone: when it happened, and codes for the kind of error and the part of the app it came from. It also notes when a run stopped while it was on the screen. It never keeps the error's message, anything you typed, a file name or anything about you, and it keeps only the most recent ones. What you write in Send feedback in About is kept there too, as a draft, until you start a new one. Neither leaves the phone unless you send it: Share crash report and Send open Android's share sheet, where you choose the app and where it goes, and each carries the app's version and build and nothing else you did not add.

Controlling the app from a computer, in this test build

This test build lets a computer connected to the phone control the app. It is protected by a code made once when the app is installed.

You should know three things about it:

  1. The code is kept unencrypted in the app's own private folder, which other apps cannot open. The Commands screen that showed it is left out of this build.
  2. The connection code is never written to the phone's log. The answers to commands, including any stored values read back, are written to the phone's system log, which a computer connected to the phone with USB debugging turned on can read. Exports you make on the Configs tab go to the app's own folder on the phone, which a computer connected to the phone can read.
  3. Every command ships, including the ones that read, export, lock and erase what the app has stored. That includes the history of your setting changes, with the numbers.

So, in this test build, an app on your phone that holds the code, or a computer with USB debugging access to your phone, can read what Euphysis has stored. That is a weakness of the test build, not an intended feature, and it is on the list of things to fix before the release.

The vault

The vault, a place for secrets typed in by hand, is left out of this build: its screen is not in the menu, and nothing in the app asks you for a secret. One trace remains: the app still declares the fingerprint or screen-lock permission, which it never asks you to use. There is no Quick Settings tile for the vault in this build.

Backups

Android's automatic backup is turned off for this app, and the app excludes every part of its storage from cloud backup and from moving to a new phone. So nothing in Euphysis's private folder is copied to your Google account, and nothing is carried across when you set up a new phone.

If you use a backup or file-sync app of your own that copies your Music folder, a plain .wav or a .mid sequence from Music/Laminae will be caught by it. That copy is outside the app's control and is governed by whatever service you used.

Testing, and what reaches us

During the test, the only information that reaches Laminae Limited is what you choose to send us, such as feedback by email. We use it only to find patterns that help us make the app better for you. We remove anything that identifies you before we analyse it, and we delete it once the analysis is done. There is never a shared central store of your data.

If you are a tester, you gave us the email address of your Google Play account. It goes into Google Play's tester list, so Google can let you install the test, and we take it off whenever you ask. Google handles the tester list and anything you choose to send through Play. That is Google's process, not the app's.

What this app never does

  • It never sends your data over a network. It holds no network permission, and no networking or upload code is written into it.
  • It shows no advertising and contains no advertising library.
  • It sends no analytics and reports no crashes to us by itself. The app keeps a small record on your phone of when you last used each of your own things, so it can list what you have not used. It is never sent.
  • It has no accounts, no sign-in and no password.
  • It does not read your location, your calendar, your health data or which other apps you use.
  • It makes no health claim and has no health features.
  • It does not sell your information. It shares nothing with us or anyone else; the only other way in is the computer connection described above.

How long things are kept, and how to delete them

Nothing the app keeps is sent anywhere. It stays on your phone until it is removed in one of these ways.

  • The Bin. When you delete a take, a sample, a sequence variant, a drawing or a picture list, it goes into the Bin. A take, sample or sequence can be restored; a drawing or a picture list cannot. After 30 days the Bin clears itself, when the app starts or the Mixer opens. Empty bin deletes a Bin's takes, samples and sequences at once, after it asks you. That cannot be undone.
  • Things you have not used. The app notes when you last used each config, sample, take, sequence, drawing and picture list. A sample, take, drawing or picture list you have not used for 60 days, that is not starred, kept or locked and is not in use, is listed under Unused. If it is still untouched 14 days later, it goes into the Bin on its own. Keep takes it off the list. Configs and sequences are only listed, never moved by the app. The check runs at most once a day.
  • History. The history of your setting changes is kept for 30 days, up to 8 MB, oldest first. Starred points are kept longer. The history holds the configs those points name, so a config you removed can come back from it for that time.
  • Configs. In this build, removing a config deletes the file at once. There is no Bin for configs.

The plain answer: uninstalling Euphysis removes everything the app keeps in its own storage. A plain .wav copy you exported, and any .mid sequence in Music/Laminae/Sequences, are ordinary files in your phone's shared storage. Delete them in your phone's own Files or Music app.

Children

Euphysis is not directed at children. On Google Play it is declared for an audience of 18 and over.

The app has no accounts, no adverts, no purchases and no messaging, and nobody can contact a person through it.

Before the microphone is used

Before Android asks for the microphone, Euphysis shows a short screen, The sound in the room, saying what it listens for and what it keeps. Euphysis shows this screen when something first needs the microphone, not when you switch the sound on. Continue goes on to Android's own dialogue; Not now, the back button or a tap outside the screen leaves the microphone off and asks nothing. If you decline Android's dialogue, the sound still plays, the microphone stays off, and the rest of the app works as before.

Your rights, and New Zealand law

Laminae Limited is a New Zealand company and registered charity, and is the agency responsible for this app under the Privacy Act 2020. Its privacy officer is Tim, the founder.

Because Euphysis holds nothing of yours on any system of ours, there is normally nothing for us to give you access to or to correct. Everything the app holds is on your own phone, where you can read it and delete it yourself, as described above.

If you believe we hold personal information about you, such as an email you sent us, you may ask for access to it and for correction of it. Write to developer-feedback@laminae.co.nz. We will answer within the time the Privacy Act allows.

If you are not satisfied with our answer, you may complain to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner (privacy.org.nz).

If something goes wrong. If we become aware of a privacy breach affecting personal information we hold, and that breach is likely to cause serious harm, we will notify the Office of the Privacy Commissioner and the people affected, as the Privacy Act 2020 requires. Because the app sends us nothing, the realistic scope of such a breach is what you have sent us directly, such as an email.
